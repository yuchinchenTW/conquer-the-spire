The fall is inside the boss fight, not on the road to it
========================================================

A climber that wins less often is either losing the last fight or walking
into it with less to fight with, and replaying whole climbs cannot tell
the two apart because each climber walks its own road. So: one reference
climber (well.pt, update 387360) walks the road and the state is saved in
each act 3 boss room, before a card is drawn; then every climber is handed
those exact states - the same health, deck, relics and potions - and
fights them out.

    python Python/cts_boss.py --fights 40 --reference runs/ironclad/well.pt \
        runs/ironclad/aim-plateau.pt ... runs/ironclad/collapsed-409827.pt

Forty fights, the reference arriving with 92% health on average:

    climber                     update    won    steps
    aim-plateau.pt              383180   65.0%    51.8
    best-look2.pt               356200   65.0%    51.0
    well.pt                     387360   62.5%    52.8
    fell-nodeep-393590.pt       393590   30.0%    54.3
    fell-397870.pt              397870   32.5%    50.6
    collapsed-409827.pt         409827    2.5%    45.5

The three healthy climbers are within two and a half points of each other
at 62-65%. The two the guard stopped are at half that, on identical
entries. The one that ran on for eight thousand updates after its fall is
at 2.5%.

So nothing about the road matters: the deck, the health and the relics
they arrive with are the same object in memory, and the difference is
entirely what they do once the fight starts. Whatever training does to
these weights, it does it to the last fight and to nothing else.

It also gives the training experiments a ten-minute read instead of a
day: a checkpoint that holds 60% here has not fallen, however its curve
looks.

A caveat the tool carries: the engine will not save inside a fight, so
the save is the boss room and the opening hand is drawn after the load.
The tests in Python/test_cts_boss.py hold that down - the same state
loaded twice and played with the same moves plays out identically, card
for card - so the draw follows the saved state rather than a fresh
shuffle.
