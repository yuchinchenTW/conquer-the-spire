===================================================

No run has beaten the 22.5% it started from. Every one has lost the third
act's boss and nothing else: the floors sit at 34, the act bosses at 93%,
the share of climbs reaching two bosses at 50%, all the way down.

The last column is the same forty saved boss rooms each time, handed to
the fallen weights - healthy ones win 60-65% of those.

What the boss shelf did
-----------------------

It worked as built. The fight went from 4.7% of a batch to 18.3%,
measured, and the first two acts lost two points each rather than their
place. It bought 1200 updates over the plain setting and left the fallen
weights a little better at the fight they lost (32.5% against 25.0%).
The climber it made also fights much longer before losing - 121 steps a
fight against 45-53 for every other set of weights - which reads as
having learnt to hold on rather than to win.

What it did not do is stop the fall or lift the ceiling. So "the fight is
too small a share of the batch" is not the whole of it: four times the
share is not four times anything.

What five runs have ruled out
-----------------------------

    the deep shelf          off is worse, not better
    the entropy pressure    held on its floor all run, fell anyway
    the value head          measured leading the policy, not leading it
    a bad batch             loss lower through the fall than before it
    the sampling            argmax and sampled fall together
    lambda                  0.98 is worse; 0.95 is what the falls use
    the share of the batch  four times as much, 1200 updates bought

What is left is the shape of the thing: a policy at 22.5% has no uphill
left that these gradients can find, and the only cheap direction from
there is to stop paying for the hardest fight. Every setting tried
changes how fast it walks downhill, not whether there is an uphill.
