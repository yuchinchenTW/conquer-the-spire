The deep shelf is not what is breaking the run
==============================================

The test was one run: carry on from well.pt (update 387360, the healthy
weights from the second collapse) with --deep 0 and nothing else changed,
and see whether the wins go past 22.5% or fall again. They fell again,
sooner: the guard stopped it at 393590, six thousand updates in, against
ten thousand with the shelf on.

    from the peak    with the shelf (0.4)      without it (0)
        +2000            20.0%                    21.2%
        +4000            19.9%                    20.2%
        +6000            19.1%                    18.8%  then 13.9%
        +8000            15.9%                    (stopped)
       +10000            12.1%

The judge agrees, on its own seeds, playing the policy flat:

    387360   25.2%      look2 36.2%
    389130   20.8%            33.0%
    391040   19.2%            39.8%
    392920   19.0%            36.5%
    393590   11.5%            24.2%

So the shelf was holding it up a little, not pushing it over - which
makes sense of the direction: without the shelf the third act is a
smaller share of what the trainer sees, and the third act's boss is the
thing that gets lost.

Three things the fall is not
----------------------------

Not a bad batch. The loss through the fall is 5.33 against 5.81 before
it, and the largest loss in the whole run (9.65) was four thousand
updates earlier, while the wins were still up.

Not the sampling. The trainer's own win share and the judge's flat
reading, which takes the best move rather than drawing one, fall
together: 21% to 13.9% on the curve, 25.2% to 11.5% in the judge.

Not the value head losing the boss fight first. Measured against what
actually followed - the climber playing its own moves, every state's
reading kept beside the discounted sum of what came after it, 180 climbs
each:

    where            well 387360                fell 393590
                  says   really   error      says   really   error
    act 1 fight   24.3    22.0    +2.3       20.4    10.0   +10.4
    act 2 fight   11.2     6.8    +4.3        6.6    -2.9    +9.5
    act 3 fight   10.8    10.1    +0.8        1.4    -5.6    +7.0
    act 3 boss     9.8     9.3    +0.5       -5.8    -6.2    +0.4

The healthy head is within a few points everywhere. The fallen one is
the other way round from what was expected: at the boss it is still
right (-5.8 against -6.2), and it is the whole rest of the climb it has
wrong, by nine or ten points. What actually followed had collapsed -
act 2 went from +6.8 to -2.9 - and the head had not caught up with its
own policy.

So the head reading -20 in boss fights on 2026-09-17 was the end of the
story and not the start of it. What starts it is still open.

What is left untested
---------------------

The entropy pressure. It is a floor that leans up whenever the policy's
spread falls under --spread (0.12), and on these weights it has leaned a
long way up: 0.032 in fights and 0.066 out of them against the 0.01 asked
for. That is a high coefficient for PPO, and it is highest exactly when
the policy has stopped improving - which is when the runs fall over. A
long boss fight is a hundred and fifty decisions with no second chance,
so it is where an extra push towards the undecided would cost most.

The test is `--spread 0`, which stops the leaning and lets every pressure
settle back on its 0.01 floor, from well.pt with nothing else changed.
