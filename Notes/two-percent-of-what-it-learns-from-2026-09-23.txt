Four runs, four peaks at the same height, and the number behind them
====================================================================

    start    shelf   spread   peak won   fell from
    383180   0.4     0.12     22.8%      +18000
    387360   0.4     0.12     22.5%      +8000
    387360   0       0.12     22.5%      +6000
    387360   0.4     0        22.6%      +11500

Four settings, four peaks inside three tenths of a point, four falls that
take only the third act's boss and leave the floors, the act bosses and
the share reaching two bosses exactly where they were. --spread 0 did
what it was asked to - the pressures sat on their 0.010 floor for the
whole run, against 0.032 before - and bought about three thousand updates
over the plain setting. It did not stop the fall and it did not lift the
ceiling.

So it is not the shelf, not the entropy pressure, not the value head
leading, not a bad batch, not the sampling. Here is what it looks like it
is.

What a batch is made of
-----------------------

The same policy, the same shelf setting, sampled moves as the trainer
takes them, six batches' worth of steps counted by where they sit:

    where              shelf off   shelf on (0.4)
    act 1 fight          23.08%      22.82%
    act 1 boss            7.57%       6.04%
    act 2 fight          30.42%      21.38%
    act 2 boss            6.15%       6.75%
    act 3 fight          11.38%      19.09%
    act 3 boss            1.74%       4.76%
    not a fight          19.66%      19.15%

The fight that every run loses is 1.74% of what the trainer learns from
without the shelf and 4.76% with it. An act 1 fight is thirteen times
more of the batch, or five times with the shelf on.

That orders the three falls: the shelf off, at 1.74%, fell soonest
(+6000); the shelf on, at 4.76%, lasted longer (+8000 and +18000). The
shelf was never the cause - it was the only thing holding the fight up.

This is catastrophic forgetting with a number on it. Ninety-five to
ninety-eight per cent of every update rewrites shared weights for the
rest of the spire, and two to five per cent has to hold a skill against
it. The skill is the last thing learnt and the first thing lost, which
is why the peak lands in the same place whatever else is changed: 22.5%
is roughly what this much of the batch can keep.

It also says why nothing tried so far moved it. A learning rate, an
entropy floor, a lambda - none of them change what fraction of the batch
the fight is.

What follows from it
--------------------

Give the fight more of the batch. The shelf already does this by
accident, keeping a snapshot of every floor of acts 2 and 3; keeping the
third act's boss room instead, or mostly, would take the fight from 4.76%
to something like a fifth of the batch without changing anything else
about the run. Python/cts_boss.py already shows the engine can save and
restore exactly that state, and reads the result in ten minutes rather
than a day.
