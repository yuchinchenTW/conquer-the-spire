Why the trainer's curve says 16% and playing says 72%
=====================================================

One set of weights - best-look2.pt, update 356200 - played five ways on
one set of 200 seeds, every climb to its end. Nothing about the weights
changes down the table; only what is done with them at the moment a move
is made.

    how it plays                          won   floors  boss  two down
    1  drawn at random, nothing walked    16.0%   33.90  92.5%   47.0%
    2  its best move, nothing walked      19.5%   34.88  93.0%   55.5%
    3  two moves walked, out of fights    35.0%   37.90  93.5%   61.5%
    4  two moves walked everywhere        43.5%   40.43  95.5%   72.5%
    5  the turn played out in fights      72.5%   44.81  98.5%   88.5%

Row 1 is what train.bat's window reports. Row 5 is what play.bat does.
The whole of the 56 points between them is spent at play time; not one
of them is the policy getting better.

Where each step goes:

  1 -> 2, three and a half points. The trainer draws a move from the
  policy rather than taking its best, because a policy that only ever
  does what it already believes learns nothing. That is the cost of
  exploring, and it is the smaller half of why the curve reads low.

  2 -> 3, fifteen and a half points. Out of a fight the best two moves
  are each walked one step on a copy and the better kept. Paths, card
  rewards, shops, fires - places where what happens next is known, so a
  single step tells the value head most of what it needs.

  3 -> 4, eight and a half points. The same one-step walk inside fights.
  Worth having and not worth much: a step into a fight is followed by
  cards nobody has drawn.

  4 -> 5, twenty-nine points. The whole turn played out instead of one
  move. This is the largest single step in the table and the reason is
  in the rules rather than the search: block only counts once the
  monsters have swung, and they swing inside the move that ends the
  turn, so one move of a turn says almost nothing about what the turn
  comes to. Nothing short of playing the turn out can see that.

The bosses are where it shows. Two bosses down goes 47% to 88.5% while
one boss down only goes 92.5% to 98.5%: the first act's boss was never
the problem, and every step down the table buys another share of the
climbs that get past the second.

What follows from it: the trainer's own curve is not a measure of how
well the climber plays, and never was. It is a measure of how well the
policy plays alone while exploring, which is the thing being trained.
Judge a training change on the curve or on cts_play --flat; judge how
good the climber is with cts_play --turn.
