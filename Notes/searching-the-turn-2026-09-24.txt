Playing the turn out before playing it
======================================

The looking cts_play does walks one move on a copy and keeps the one the
value head thinks more of. Measured, that pays out of a fight and barely
pays in one - and the reason is that a turn is a sequence. Block only
counts once the monsters have swung, and they swing inside the move that
ends the turn, so the first card of a turn says almost nothing about what
the turn comes to.

So: cts_turn.py grows sequences a move at a time from the state at hand,
keeps the best few by what the value head says, and stops when the turn
ends or the budget runs out. A sequence is worth what it paid along the
way, discounted by when, plus what the head says about where it left the
climb, discounted by how long it was. Ending the turn is a move like any
other, so a sequence that ends it is scored after the monsters have
answered - which is the whole point.

Three things it does not do, each one learnt the hard way:

  * It does not enumerate the turn. Five cards with targets is thousands
    of orders and most differ by nothing. The budget is sequences walked
    (120 a decision), not depth, so a hand with one sensible line is
    cheap.
  * It does not trust the head far. Looking at four candidates one move
    ahead beat looking at two nowhere and lost floors in a fight - the
    widest search finds the head's largest error, not the board's best
    line. Four sequences carried, four moves considered from each.
  * It does not run out of a fight, where the one-move looking already
    wins.

What it is worth, on the forty saved boss rooms of cts_boss.py, which
nothing trains on:

    climber                     names its move    searches the turn
    well.pt (387360)                62.5%              77.5%
    fell-bossshelf (400070)         32.5%              55.0%

Fifteen points on the healthy weights and twenty-two on the fallen ones.
Forty fights carry a standard error of about seven points, so the size is
rough and the direction is not: this is the first thing tried in five
experiments that has moved the last fight at all.

The fallen weights are the more interesting half. Playing their own moves
they fight 121 steps before losing, against 45-53 for every other set of
weights - they had learnt to hold on rather than to win. With the search
in front of them that falls to 52.6 and the wins rise to 55%: the skill
was not gone from the weights, it was gone from the moves they named.

The engine side is VecSpireEnv::Walk - a sequence taken in order on a
copy, saying what each move paid and where it landed - and LoadOne, so a
saved room can be handed to a row. Both have tests.

Still to do: whole climbs. The boss rooms say the fight is winnable; only
cts_play over a fixed seed set says whether a climb is.
